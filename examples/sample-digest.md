**Huberman Lab：睡眠剝奪的代價**

史丹佛大學神經科學家 Andrew Huberman 與睡眠研究先驅 Matt Walker 博士共同探討了一個讓人不安的事實：大多數人對自己的睡眠不足程度已嚴重缺乏感知。研究顯示，長期睡眠不足（每晚低於七小時）不僅損害認知功能，更會大幅提高焦慮症與憂鬱症的罹患風險。Walker 的研究表明，睡眠中的 REM 階段是情緒調節的關鍵，剝奪這一階段就等於剝奪了大腦的「情緒急救」機制。

Stanford neuroscientist Andrew Huberman and sleep researcher Matt Walker unpack a
counterintuitive finding: people who are chronically sleep-deprived lose the ability
to accurately assess how impaired they are. After two weeks of six hours per night,
subjects felt fine — but performed at the level of someone who had been awake for
24 hours straight. Walker argues that REM sleep is the brain's overnight therapy
session for processing emotional experiences. Miss enough of it, and the emotional
bruises start to accumulate.
https://youtube.com/watch?v=example_huberman_walker

"Sleep is the single most effective thing we can do to reset our brain and body health
each day."
「睡眠是我們每天能做的最有效的事情，用來重置大腦和身體的健康。」
— Matthew Walker, Why We Sleep (2017)

**Naval Ravikant：財富不等於金錢**

Naval 在一段廣為流傳的錄音中提出了一個讓許多人重新思考努力方向的觀點：真正的財富是資產，而不是薪資。他區分了兩種收入模式：出租時間（薪資）和擁有資產（投資、知識產品、品牌）。「你不可能透過出租時間變得非常富有，」他說。財富的關鍵在於建立能在你睡覺時繼續產生價值的事物。這個框架不僅適用於金融，也適用於知識和影響力的積累。

Naval Ravikant draws a sharp line between getting rich and earning a salary. Wealth,
he argues, is ownership — of assets that work while you sleep: equity, code, content,
or distribution. "Judgment, leverage, and specific knowledge are the tools. Hard work
is overrated." His key insight is that most people are optimizing for income when they
should be optimizing for ownership. The distinction sounds simple but reshapes how you
think about every hour spent.

**Lenny's Newsletter：為什麼頂尖 PM 思考方式不同**

Lenny Rachitsky 訪談了十位前谷歌、Airbnb、Stripe 的產品副總裁後，歸納出一個共同特徵：頂尖 PM 不問「我們應該建什麼？」，而是問「為什麼用戶在我們的產品中遇到這個問題？」這種從根本原因出發的思維方式，使他們在做功能決策時比一般 PM 快且準確得多。文章中最值得記住的框架是：每個功能請求背後都藏著一個真正的問題，你的工作是找到那個問題，而不是建造那個功能。

Lenny Rachitsky's interviews with 10 VP-level PMs surfaced a pattern: the best product
managers systematically refuse to take feature requests at face value. When an engineer
asks for a dashboard, a top PM asks what decision the user can't make without it. This
"five-whys" instinct, applied consistently, is what separates product leaders from
product order-takers. One interviewee's rule: never write a spec for a feature until you
can articulate the specific user frustration in one sentence.
